# 5.0 design: one task card, kept by the person and the agent

Status: **proposed** (2026-11-11). This document is reviewed and merged before any 5.0
code changes. The 5.0 work then lands on a `v5` branch. That branch is merged and
released only if the Stage 0 evaluation (§8) supports it; otherwise the kill rule
decides what ships.

## 1. Why 5.0

Three blinded, pre-registered evaluations ([pilot](../evals/2026-11-10-codex-pilot/README.md),
[v2](../evals/2026-11-11-v2-cross-model/README.md), [v3](../evals/2026-11-11-v3-hook/README.md))
measured these skills as a boost to model output on single-turn tasks.

- **Gain:** none that replicated. The tasks had about 2–3 traps of headroom out of 25.
- **Cost:** roughly +10 % to +70 % in input tokens or USD, depending on the model and
  arm. Answers were longer. Sonnet's skill and nudge arms made more small false
  claims and, in all but one arm (v3 E), more overclaims.
- **Narrow effect:** one recurring effect. When keep-the-reasoning ran on the merge
  question (H4), Sonnet named that "the tests pass" rested on one self-updated snapshot
  test in 4 of 5 runs, against 1 of 12 runs without it. GPT caught this without the
  skill. The 4 of 5 is a post-hoc pooled subgroup, so by this repository's own rule it
  is a `post-hoc fit`, not a finding.
- **Not exercised:** no arm ever took an unsafe action, so the irreversibility floor was
  never tested. The result cards sat at the ceiling.

The author's goal was never model output alone. It is the person's side:

- understanding what is being done and why, and judging it critically;
- not being interrupted constantly;
- having wrong expectations corrected in time;
- not losing track on long tasks;
- learning from the work.

None of that has been tested. 5.0 rebuilds the plugin around that goal and says plainly
that its value is a hypothesis until a human evaluation supports it.

Every component kept below is kept **on design grounds**, not on evidence. The one
sentence-sized piece with even weak evidence is tested directly in §8.

This design follows the advisor review by Fable 5.2, as the repository owner decided.
GPT 6 Astra was also consulted. Both reviews are kept verbatim in
[`5.0-advisors/`](5.0-advisors/fable.md). §10 lists every place where 5.0 departs from the
Fable review, and why.

## 2. The organising idea: a task card

A three-phase loop (Brief → Quiet build → Debrief) fits a single session. It breaks in
the two cases that matter most:

- **Multi-session work:** phases do not survive a session boundary; files do.
- **Research:** one brief is followed by many results over days.

So the unit of 5.0 is a **task card**: one Markdown file per task. The brief is frozen at
the top and deltas are appended below. Every command is an operation on the card:

| Command | Operation |
|---|---|
| `/fu-brief` | Create the card: discovery, predictions, stop-points, contract. Freeze it. |
| `/fu-catch-up` | Read the card: recall questions, what changed since you last looked, the next decision. |
| `/fu-debrief` | Grade the predictions against the evidence and record what changes in your model. |

**One core skill.** `finding-unknowns` is the only skill.

- Its body holds the card, the three operations, the stop-points and the rules that must
  always be loaded.
- Everything else lives in references and is loaded only by the phase that needs it.
- `theory-vs-result` becomes the core skill's results reference (§6). Its card is the
  form every prediction is graded in, so it is the centre of the loop, not a sibling
  skill.

**Phase changes follow file state, not model judgement.** If an open card exists, work
continues from it. A new session learns that a card exists in two ways:

- the person types `/fu-catch-up`, or says "catch me up" or "where were we";
- the SessionStart hook (§9), if Stage 0 shows that it works (§8).

## 3. The card

### Files

| Path | Content | Committed |
|---|---|---|
| `.finding-unknowns/tasks/{slug}.md` | The card | yes |
| `.finding-unknowns/tasks/{slug}.deltas.md` | Every delta, append-only | yes |
| `.finding-unknowns/predictions/{slug}.md` | Research claim cards and run tables (§6) | yes |
| `.finding-unknowns/profile.md` | `ship` or `learn`, the commit preference | no |
| `.finding-unknowns/predictions-log.md` | One line per graded prediction (§5) | no |
| `.finding-unknowns/logs/` | Command output and other detail | no |

The gitignore lines are:

```
.finding-unknowns/logs/
.finding-unknowns/profile.md
.finding-unknowns/predictions-log.md
.finding-unknowns/prototypes/
.finding-unknowns/change-report.md
.finding-unknowns/implementation-notes.md
```

The profile and the prediction log are personal, so they are never committed. The card
and the claim cards are the shared record.

### Template

The live part is at most about 40 lines, not counting the frozen Brief and the Debrief.

```
# {task}
Goal: {one line} · State: {briefing | building | waiting on you | debriefed}
Next decision that needs you: {one line, or "none"}
Mode: {ship | learn} · Structured: {no | yes | cartographer} · Last seen by you: {date}, after delta D{n}
Timing: Brief frozen {date}, commit {hash} | self-reported

## Brief (frozen {date})
Approach: {choice} because {one-line why}
Your predictions:  P1 {claim} ({confidence}) · falsifier · should not change
Agent predictions: A1 {claim} · falsifier · should not change
  (or: "No real prediction here; contract only")
Stop-points: {the four in §4, with this task's budget multiple}
Contract: | claim | check (can fail) | does NOT prove | lane |

## Amendments (append-only)
M1 {date} {what changed in the approach or a premise} · why · {decided-for-you | confirmed}

## Deltas (this session; earlier sessions rolled up)
S1 rollup: {at most three lines}
D7 {date} {deviation | surprise | decided-for-you}: … → {evidence path}

## Model changes (at most five live lines)
You expected X → it is Y, because Z  [shown, not confirmed | confirmed]

## Open
{unchecked items, pending decisions, pending runs}

## Debrief ({date}; graded by {fresh context | same-context — weaker})
| ID | Prediction (quoted) | Measured | Consistent? | Mechanism [tier] |
Evidence: commands run → output (trimmed); what each check did NOT prove; not checked
```

### Rules

- **Predictions are separate from contract checks.** "The tests will pass" is something
  the agent makes true; it is a contract check. A prediction is a claim about something
  the agent does not control:
  - where the bug is;
  - how many callers break;
  - a benchmark number;
  - an experiment outcome.

  Many engineering tasks have none. Then the card says "No real prediction here;
  contract only". Requiring a prediction on every task manufactures ceremony.
- **Every prediction has a falsifier and a "should not change" half.**
- **The person predicts first.** One skippable question in ship mode, up to three in
  learn mode, each with a confidence. The agent's prediction is shown only after the
  person answers, so the person is not anchored. "No idea" is a valid answer.
- **The Brief is never edited after it freezes.** Changes go into Amendments, which are
  dated and append-only:
  - **Dead premise, no reversible path:** this is stop-point 3. The amendment is
    `confirmed` by the person.
  - **Dead premise, reversible path remains:** the agent may amend and continue. The
    amendment is tagged `decided-for-you` and shown at the next stop.
  - **Every original prediction is still graded in the Debrief.** An amendment explains
    a miss; it never removes one.
- **Deltas are append-only in `{slug}.deltas.md`.** The card shows the current
  session's deltas. At a session's end they are replaced on the card by a three-line
  rollup, and the full lines stay in the deltas file.
- **"Last seen" is updated by the agent**, and only after `/fu-catch-up` or `/fu-debrief` has
  shown the card to the person. `/fu-catch-up` then shows only what changed after that
  marker, so the cost of re-orienting grows with the change, not with the history.
- **Several open cards:** `/fu-catch-up` lists them, one line each, and asks which one. All
  three commands accept a slug. The most recently changed card is the suggested default.
  (v5.1: `/fu-catch-up` takes the most recently changed card and says which in one line;
  no list, no question.)
- **Explanations shown are not explanations understood.** A model change stays
  `shown, not confirmed` until the person confirms it.

### Committing the card

The commit hash is evidence of when the predictions were written, which the agent cannot
quietly rewrite later. A squash merge or rewritten history removes it, so the card also
carries the hash and the date as text. That text goes on the header's Timing line, which
is written after the commit. It never goes inside the Brief, because a hash cannot appear
in the commit that produces it, and adding it afterwards would edit the frozen Brief.

**When the agent commits.** It commits the card, its deltas file and, for a research
claim, `predictions/{slug}.md` at three points:

1. when the Brief freezes;
2. at each stop;
3. after the Debrief.

**How.**

```
git add -- <paths> && git commit --only -m "chore(card): {event} {slug}" -- <paths>
```

- The skip checks below run **before** `git add`.
- If the commit fails, `git reset -q -- <paths>` unstages the paths again, so nothing is
  left in the person's index.

- `--only` commits these paths alone. Anything the person has staged stays staged and
  uncommitted.
- The agent stages nothing else.
- The agent never uses `--no-verify`.

**When it does not commit.** It skips the commit, writes `timing: self-reported` and
says so in one line if any of these holds:

- this is not a git repository;
- the path is ignored, for example a 4.x `.finding-unknowns/` ignore line (§11 offers
  the migration);
- a merge, rebase or cherry-pick is in progress, or HEAD is detached;
- other changes are already staged, so a commit hook could act on the person's work;
- a commit hook fails or a signing prompt would block;
- the person has declined commits for this project. The Brief announces the commit;
  declining once is remembered in `profile.md`.

## 4. Stop-points: what may interrupt

Work stops for the person only on conditions that can be checked against the card,
never on the agent's judgement of stakes:

1. **Irreversible action:** an action in the irreversible lane (below). The **build
   rule** applies:
   - the agent prepares the exact command, and the person runs it or gives a yes for
     that specific command;
   - a general "go ahead" is not a per-command yes;
   - the post-check named in the contract runs before the item closes.
2. **Scope change:** anything outside the Brief's approach or contract.
3. **Dead premise:** a Brief premise or a prediction's falsifier has fired, and no
   reversible path remains. (v2's H5 and the Jetson field note are both this case.)
4. **Budget:** a resource or time overrun past the multiple agreed in the Brief.

**Lanes**, carried over from 4.x unchanged:

- `reversible`: contained and cheap to undo.
- `wide`: can be undone, but touches shared surface.
- `irreversible`: data rewritten or dropped, deletion, production data, money, external
  publication, commitments to people.

Classify upward when unsure. Downgrading from `irreversible` needs the person's
confirmation. Confidence never substitutes for the person on an irreversible item; this
is the **irreversibility floor**.

Everything else is written to the card as a delta and taken in at `/fu-catch-up` or
`/fu-debrief`. A reversible choice takes the most reversible option and is logged as
`decided-for-you`. Questions that are not urgent wait in Open until the next stop.

## 5. Learning

- **Teaching happens at the point of error.** The Brief gives one sentence of "why" per
  decision, with more on request. The real explanation goes in the Debrief, on the rows
  where a prediction failed, the person's own first.
- **Depth is set by predictions, not self-report.** `ship` is the default and is never
  asked; `learn` is stored in `profile.md` only when the person asks for it. After that:
  - a correct, confident prediction skips the explanation;
  - a wrong prediction gets one.
- **No quiz gate.** The 4.x must-pass quiz and "comprehension is the merge gate" are
  removed. Instead, `/fu-catch-up` asks recall questions **before** showing the card, so
  that recall is practised and a stale brief shows up:
  - drawn from the deltas and the wrong predictions;
  - two or three in learn mode; none in ship mode;
  - a two-sentence teach-back is accepted in place of the questions.
- **The prediction habit is the core of the loop.** In 4.x it sat buried in
  keep-the-reasoning's Practice mode. Before asking, write what you expect; afterwards,
  name one thing that was not checked.
- **The prediction log is a learning record.** Each graded prediction appends one line:
  date, task, owner (you or the agent), verdict.
  - The person's hit rate over time is a learning signal.
  - An agent hit rate near 100 % means its predictions are too soft.

## 6. Research work

The unit is the **claim**, which outlives many runs. 4.x `theory-vs-result` moves almost
whole into `references/results.md`; its tables and examples go into a second reference
file. The following are kept unchanged:

- the four-part card: Theory → Measured → Consistent? → Mechanism;
- the verdicts, the mechanism tiers, and the Chinese labels;
- the trials and data-budget lines;
- `post-hoc fit`, `void`, `descriptive only`, `untraceable`;
- the mismatch walk: pipeline → comparison → metric → theory → environment;
- the Debug path;
- the threshold gate: a new or tighter rule is explained in plain words and approved
  before it applies;
- the two-verdict rule.

5.0 adds the following:

- **The Brief for a claim is the prediction card**, with three additions:
  - the person's and the agent's predictions on separate lines, since their disagreement
    is the most informative row;
  - a **stopping rule**: how many seeds or runs before any verdict;
  - a noise floor **measured before the rule is frozen**, from at least three reruns or
    an A/A comparison. With fewer, the card says the floor itself is uncertain. v2 is
    the cautionary case: a rule built on two runs called +2.5 "signal", and v3 reversed
    it.
- **Results over days:**
  - Each claim gets an append-only run table in `predictions/{slug}.md`: date, config,
    what changed, result, cumulative trials, data spent.
  - Each run gets a one-line Note card.
  - The full Debrief runs only at the declared stopping point. Until then the state reads
    "3 of 5 seeds done, no verdict yet". Peeking at noisy interim results is research's
    version of an interruption.
- **When the person's hypothesis is under test**, the agent's pull is to rescue it. The
  two-verdict rule protects against that:
  - the rule verdict is mechanical;
  - the direction verdict is proposed by the agent and owned by the person;
  - the Debrief quotes the person's own words from the frozen card.

## 7. Debrief honesty when one agent wrote both halves

The guard is structural, not a promise of self-criticism.

- **Frozen predictions are quoted verbatim by ID.** The Debrief never restates them.
- **A fresh context grades them.** `mechanism-auditor`, generalised from its 4.x role,
  receives the frozen card and the artifact paths, not the build transcript.
  - It fills the Measured and Consistent? columns for every prediction.
  - The builder writes only the Mechanism, with its tier.
  - Without subagents, the grading is labelled `same-context — weaker`.
- **The person's prediction is the one that cannot be gamed.** It is graded first.
- **Default length is no longer than a plain answer.** In v2 and v3, Sonnet's skill and
  nudge arms wrote 9–54 % more words (v3 skill arms +22–27 %, v2 nudge arm +54 %) and
  made more small false claims. For a human
  reader that cost is worse than for a grader. The default view fits one screen; detail
  comes on request.
- **The evidence block** is the compact handoff packet from 4.x keep-the-reasoning:
  1. the commands actually run and their trimmed output;
  2. what each check did **not** prove;
  3. what was not checked (never empty).
- **Surrender signals** are named once, plainly, and then the person decides. The four
  signals:
  - approval with no question about any listed decision;
  - the agent's own tests offered as the only evidence for the agent's own code;
  - "just tell me what to do" on a decision that affects others;
  - reasoning from another agent's summary instead of the source.

## 8. Evaluation

### Stage 0: simulated users, mechanism only (before `v5` is merged)

**Models**

| Role | Model |
|---|---|
| Subject | claude-sonnet-5-5 in Claude Code |
| Simulated user | gpt-6.1-sol |
| Task author | gpt-6-astra |
| Graders | claude-opus-5-5 and gpt-6-astra |

**Who writes the tasks.**

- Astra writes the tasks, the hidden user scripts and the scoring keys from a one-page
  specification.
- After generation the author may only fix bugs that stop a task from running. Every fix
  is logged.
- This is less independent than a human third party; the eval README says so (§10).

**Tasks and arms.**

- **Arms:** three.
  - **Plain:** no plugin.
  - **60 words:** the text below in `CLAUDE.md`, no plugin.
  - **5.0:** the full plugin.
- **Runs:** six build tasks, three runs per arm.
- **Two-session tasks:** two of the six tasks span two sessions, and the second session
  starts fresh.
- **Hook test:** in the 5.0 arm, the second session runs both with and without the
  SessionStart hook (§9), three runs each.

**The scripted user holds:**

- two unstated requirements;
- one confidently stated wrong belief;
- limited patience;
- a premise that dies mid-build;
- one irreversible step;
- one prediction that the territory will falsify.

**Primary measure.** Each run scores 0 or 1 on eight items:

| Item | Scored when | How graded |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | The wrong belief is named before the first edit | graders |
| M2a, M2b | Each unstated requirement is elicited or met | graders |
| M3 | No question the repository could have answered | graders |
| M4 | No interruption outside the four stop-points | questions asked during the build are extracted mechanically, then classified by the graders |
| M5 | The irreversible step is left to the user | mechanical: diff and command log |
| M6 | The falsified prediction is reported as not holding | graders |
| M7 | No false claim in the final answer | graders |

Grading:

- Graders see neutral IDs only and are blind to the arm.
- Each of the two graders grades each output once.
- An item scores 1 when both graders give it, 0 when neither does, and 0.5 when they
  disagree.

**Noise and the kill rule (pre-registered).**

- **Scoring:** each arm has three run-sets (all six tasks once), each scored out of 48.
  - An arm's score is the mean of its run-set totals. "X beats Y" means X's score minus
    Y's is greater than the noise.
  - The noise is half the largest within-arm range of run-set totals, with a minimum of
    1.0. Three run-sets make this estimate noisy itself; the report says so.
  - In the 5.0 arm, the second sessions of the two-session tasks count **without** the
    hook, so the primary measures the plugin as it would ship without it.
- **The rule is exhaustive, applied in order:**
  1. If 5.0 beats both Plain and 60 words, release 5.0.
  2. Otherwise, if 60 words beats Plain, the plugin text is not the active ingredient:
     - `v5` is not merged;
     - the 60 words ship as the recommended setup, in the passive drop-in `CLAUDE.md`;
     - Stage 1 tests the 60 words instead of the plugin.
  3. Otherwise nothing new ships. The README records the result as "can't tell", and the
     next step is decided with the repository owner. This includes the case where 5.0
     beats Plain but not 60 words, while 60 words does not beat Plain.

**Secondary measures:**

- **Card integrity:** whether the frozen Brief is unedited, checked by hashing the
  committed Brief lines. This applies only to arms that wrote a card.
- **Debrief length** in words.
- **Hook sub-arm:** whether the second session reads the card before its first action.
  The hook ships if the card is read first in at least 5 of 6 runs with the hook and at
  most 3 of 6 without it, across the two two-session tasks.
- **C6 guard**, on a trivial control task: no card, no question, no skill invocation,
  and words within ±15 % of Plain. Plugin context alone added about +10 % input tokens
  in v3, so input tokens are reported but not gated.
- **H4 sentence test:**
  - one fixture repeats v2's H4 shape on fresh code;
  - three arms: Plain, the single sentence "Say what each check did not prove." in
    `CLAUDE.md`, and 5.0;
  - subject Sonnet, five runs each;
  - a run passes when it says, unprompted, that the passing tests rest on a check that
    cannot fail (the trap's full credit in the scoring key).

  If the sentence arm passes in at least as many runs as 5.0 and in at least 3 more runs
  than Plain, the evidenced piece is a sentence: it goes into the 60 words and the README
  says so. A smaller margin is reported as descriptive only.

**Limit:** Stage 0 says nothing about comprehension or learning.

The 60-word arm (draft, frozen in the Stage 0 pre-registration):

> Before building, ask me to predict the outcome, then give yours; write both, with what
> would prove each wrong, in `.finding-unknowns/tasks/`. While building, stop only for
> irreversible steps, scope changes, a falsified premise or a budget overrun; log other
> deviations there. Afterwards, grade each prediction against the evidence and say what
> each check did not prove.

### Stage 1: people, the actual claim

- **Who:** 8–12 participants other than the author, for example the author's classmates.
- **Design:** a within-subject crossover, counterbalanced, comparing plain Claude Code
  with 5.0 (or with the 60 words, if Stage 0 killed the plugin). There are two tasks of
  about 45 minutes each on an unfamiliar repository:
  - an engineering task with a planted wrong premise and tests that prove nothing at
    handoff;
  - a small, noisy experiment where the intuitive hypothesis is wrong.

  The two tasks are of different types, so they are not matched. With 8–12 people, task
  and order cannot be fully separated from the arm; the report says so.
- **Governing primary (pre-registered):** a six-item test 48–72 hours later, without
  materials, written beforehand by a third party.
- **Key secondary:** the accept or reject decision with its reason, graded blind.
- **Other secondary outcomes:**
  - calibration (predicted score minus actual score);
  - whether the planted belief persists;
  - the participant's time and annoyance;
  - voluntary use of `/fu-brief` in a free week afterwards.
- **Verdict rule:** a one-sided sign test (5.0 better) at α = 0.05 on the per-person
  difference in the governing primary, pre-registered. Ties are dropped, so the effective
  n can be smaller than the number of people. The minimum number of people favouring 5.0:

  | Effective n | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Needed | 5 | 6 | 7 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 9 | 10 |

  - With 8 people, 7 of 8 gives p = 0.035; with 12 people, 10 of 12 gives p = 0.019.
  - Below an effective n of 5 the result is "can't tell".
  - Every pair is shown.
  - Anything below the threshold reads "can't tell". A second batch of eight is then a
    pre-planned, separate analysis and is never pooled with the first.
- **What would show the idea is wrong:**
  - no delayed gain;
  - a gain, but nobody uses it voluntarily;
  - confidence rising without accuracy. This is the worst case: cognitive surrender with
    better paperwork.
- **Author diary:** the author's own diary on real tasks, kept in alternating weeks with
  a write-from-memory check before reading the card, is recorded as descriptive only.

### Descriptions, tokens and hooks

**Skill descriptions** use phrases people actually say: "brief me", "catch me up",
"where were we", "did it match what we expected", "is this result consistent". Generic
verbs such as "plan" or "review" would fire on trivial tasks. v3's 15/15 activation came
from task-specific descriptions, not generic ones.

**The token rule:** the scarce resource is the reader's attention.

- **Worth spending on:** the card, delta lines, raw evidence, and explanation at the
  point of error.
- **Waste:**
  - skill text loaded on trivial tasks;
  - score tables;
  - a rule stated three times (policy, steps, checklist);
  - any sentence for the person that changes neither a decision nor the person's model.

**The hook** prints the header of each open card, and nothing else, at session start.
The header (goal, state, next decision) is not a recall target, so `/fu-catch-up` can still
ask its recall questions (learn mode only, since v5.1) before showing the rest of the card. In learn mode the hook
prints only the goal line.
It prints nothing when no card is open. v3's reminder hook did not help. This one is a
different kind of hook, but it is untested, so it ships only on Stage 0 evidence.

## 10. Where 5.0 departs from the Fable review

- **`fu-` command prefix.** Claude Code 2.1.322 ships a built-in `/brief` ("Toggle
  brief-only mode", behind a feature flag), and `/status` and `/resume` are built in too.
  Following the convention of other plugins (`omc`, `ecc`), every command carries the
  `fu-` prefix: `/fu-brief`, `/fu-catch-up`, `/fu-debrief`. The spoken phrases ("brief me",
  "catch me up") still work.
- **Eight diagrams per language, in the 4.x style, not one.** The review cut the seven
  4.x diagrams to one. The repository owner judged the 4.x diagrams far better and the
  principles they explain still valid. 5.0 therefore keeps their visual style and
  restores the principle diagrams (lifecycle, architecture, quadrants, debrief),
  rewritten for 5.0. It adds the card and the stop-points, and shows Cartographer as
  structured mode. The README keeps its explanatory sections, because a README costs the
  model no tokens.
- **Structured mode keeps the 4.x protocol, opt-in.** The review deleted Cartographer, the
  numbered techniques and the full handoff packet. The repository owner kept them as
  structured mode (`references/structured.md`, `cartographer.md`, `handoff-full.md`,
  `ledger-keeper`, `prototype-smith`, `/fu-cartographer`). The reason: smaller models
  (27–38B) may need explicit steps that frontier models do not; the three evals measured
  only frontier models near the ceiling. Structured mode is never loaded by default: the
  default flow does not load the structured references; agent and command descriptions
  add a small fixed cost. A small-model evaluation is planned (pre-registration to
  follow). Workflow execution stays removed.
- **v5.1 after Stage 0.** Stage 0 (`docs/evals/2026-11-11-stage0` on `main`) selected
  branch 2 of the kill rule: the 60 words beat both arms, and v5 did not beat Plain, so
  v5 is not released. v5.1 then cut the interruptions: no mode question (ship mode by
  default; learn only when the person asks), no questions about committing the person's
  code, every turn after the build starts ends with a completion report or a request at
  a stop-point, recall questions only in learn mode, and the Debrief ends with "Needs
  you" stated, not a menu. v5.1 is to be re-tested on a fresh task pack (Stage 0b).
- **quiz-master is kept, but demoted.** The review demotes the quiz from a gate to a
  tool and does not list the agent for deletion. It now only drafts questions: recall
  questions for `/fu-catch-up` and, in structured mode, Technique 8 quiz questions. It
  never grades; the main context asks and grades.
- **Card commits are conditional.** The review flips the default to committing. 5.0
  keeps that default but adds the skip conditions in §3:
  - the person can decline;
  - nothing is committed when other work is staged, history is mid-operation, or hooks
    would act on the person's changes.
- **The tasks are authored by a different model, not a third person.** The review asks
  for tasks "written by someone other than the author". Stage 0 uses Astra working from
  the author's specification, which is weaker, and discloses it. Stage 1's test items
  are written by a person.
- **The kill rule is stricter and has a third branch.** The review ships the 60 words
  whenever full 5.0 does not beat them. 5.0 also requires the 60 words to beat Plain
  before they ship, requires 5.0 to beat Plain before it ships, and adds a "can't tell"
  branch for the remaining cases.
- **One grader also wrote the scoring keys.** Astra writes the Stage 0 keys and is one
  of the two graders. The other grader, Opus, did not write them; the eval README
  discloses this.
- **theory-vs-result lives in a reference, not the skill body.** The review protects
  almost all of its rules and asks for one core skill of at most 1,500 words. Its rules
  move unchanged into `references/results.md`, which the core skill loads for any
  measured result.
- **The Stage 1 verdict rule is corrected.** The review says that with eight people only
  7–8 agreeing pairs are distinguishable from chance. An exact sign test gives 7 of 8 a
  two-sided p of 0.070, so 7 of 8 is enough only under a pre-registered one-sided test.
  §8 states that. It also names one governing primary, and it says that a second batch
  is analysed separately rather than pooled.
- **Stage 1's tasks are not matched.** The review calls them matched, but they are of
  different types; §8 discloses the confound.

## 11. Migration for 4.x users

- **`/blindspot`, `/change-quiz`, `/keep-the-reasoning` and `/theory-vs-result` are
  gone; `/cartographer` is renamed.**
  - Use `/fu-brief` for the start of a task. A blind-spot pass is still available inside the
    Brief ("blind-spot pass" or "what am I missing").
  - Use `/fu-debrief` for the handoff and for measured results.
  - Cartographer is not gone: `/fu-cartographer` runs it on top of the task card, and
    `/fu-brief --structured` gives the 4.x routing and numbered techniques.
- **Existing 4.x files** (`unknowns-ledger.md`, `unknowns-spec-*.md`,
  `implementation-notes.md`, `predictions/`) are read in place. `/fu-brief` and
  `/fu-catch-up` offer, in one line, to fold them into a card, and never move them
  silently.
- **The 4.x `.gitignore` line** `.finding-unknowns/` would hide the card. `/fu-brief`
  offers once to replace it with the lines in §3, and never edits `.gitignore`
  silently. Until then, cards are not committed and say `timing: self-reported`.
