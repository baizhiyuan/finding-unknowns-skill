## 1. The essence

**What it is for.** The owner delegates work and decisions to an assistant every day. The skill exists so that each of those exchanges leaves the owner knowing something they did not know, and still holding the judgment. It does that by keeping one thing visible: **the person's own map of what they know and do not know, updated only on evidence.**

**How the two sources become one idea.**

- The four quadrants are the map. They say where an unknown lives: in what you rely on (KK), in a question you can name (KU), in something you know but have not said (UK), or in something you do not know to ask (UU).
- The critical-thinking habits are the rules for updating the map.
  - *Predict before you look* records your map before the assistant's answer overwrites it. A missed prediction shows that a "known" was wrong.
  - *Say what a check did not prove* stops a claim entering "known" further than its evidence reaches.
  - *Say what is still unknown* keeps the open part of the map visible after the work looks finished.
- Osmani's slide (offloading → deferral → cognitive surrender) is what happens when the map stops being kept. In surrender, the assistant's output becomes your "known" without any update step. Each habit above interrupts that at one point.

So the unit is not a task, a card or a phase. It is **the map, shown twice**: before the work (where the unknowns are) and after it (what moved).

**Smallest set of behaviours.**

| # | Behaviour | Source | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Before non-trivial work, show the map in four fields, with the person's expectation quoted if they gave one | Thariq's quadrants; Osmani's "write down what the answer should look like" | Stage 0: the short text named the person's wrong belief before the first edit in 0.94 of runs, against 0.11 plain |
| 2 | Ask only what only the person can answer, with a recommended answer; find everything else out | v4 "never ask what the territory can answer"; gateguard | gateguard: 0 of 48 trials passed without the fact only the human held; agents already find repository facts |
| 3 | Work quietly; stop only for a scope change, a falsified premise or an irreversible step | 5.x stop-points | Stage 0 M4: short text 0.92, plugin 0.11; Tier 1 TW1 and TW2 passed for the 82 words (TW1 under the detector's reading only) |
| 4 | Afterwards, show what moved: expectation against evidence, now known, not proved, still unknown | keep-the-reasoning compact packet; theory-vs-result | The "did not prove" step caught a hollow test result in 4 of 5 invoked runs against 1 of 12 (eval v2/v3, as quoted in `SITUATION.md`) |
| 5 | On the person's request: interview them; look back over the record | Thariq's interview; the owner's addendum | None measured. The owner reports the interview is useful to them |

Behaviours 1–4 are the always-on text. Behaviour 5 is the skill.

**Not established by any of this:** that a person understands or learns more. Every number above comes from simulated users or single-turn tasks.

## 4. The four quadrants, made practical

**Before = the map. After = what moved on it.**

| Quadrant | What it means for the person | The assistant's move | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **KK** known knowns | What you rely on. The danger is a false known | List the few facts and beliefs the work rests on, each with its source. Check the ones that can be checked. Mark any that turned out wrong | Before; the correction shows again after |
| **KU** known unknowns | A question you can name | Split it: "I will find out" (and how) or "needs you" (with a recommended answer) | Before; resolved ones return after as "now known" |
| **UK** unknown knowns | What you know but did not say: taste, constraints, context | Guess it out loud so you can react: "I assume you want X because Y". A reaction is cheaper for you than a specification | Before; in the interview, two contrasting options to react to |
| **UU** unknown unknowns | What you did not know to ask | Up to three things that matter and that you did not ask about, each with why it matters. "None found" is a valid entry | Before; afterwards, new questions the work raised go under "still unknown" |

### Terminal template: before

```
fu:before · {task, at most 10 words}
- You expect: "{their words}" | not stated
- KK · relying on
  - {fact or belief} — {you said | file:line | source | assumed}
  - ✗ {belief that the check contradicted} — {evidence}
- KU · open
  - {question} — I will find out by {how}
  - {question} — needs you; I would pick {X} because {reason}
- UK · I assume you want
  - {guess at an unstated preference or constraint} — {why I guess this}
- UU · you did not ask, but it matters
  - {blind spot} — {why it matters}
- I expect: {outcome}; wrong if {what would show it}
```

Rules:

- At most 12 lines. An empty field is omitted, except `You expect`, which always prints.
- If KK holds nothing surprising and KU, UK and UU are empty, print one line: `fu:before · no significant unknowns`. Do not manufacture entries (v4: "say it plainly rather than manufacturing concerns").
- **Stop after this block only if** an entry under "needs you" or UK guards something irreversible or costly to redo, or a `✗` contradicts what the person asked for. Otherwise continue in the same turn on the recommended answers.

### Capturing the person's prediction without interrupting

1. **Take it from the request.** "I think the bug is in the parser; fix it" already holds a prediction. Quote it.
2. **The empty slot is the nudge.** When none was stated, the block prints `You expect: not stated`. The assistant does not stop for it. The person sees the empty slot each time and learns to put the guess in the request. This is a guess about what builds the habit; §8 checks it.
3. **Ask only when already stopping, or when the work ends in an irreversible step or a decision the person will act on.** Then the ask comes first in the message, and the assistant's own expectation is withheld until the person answers or skips: `I expect: held until you answer or skip`. Showing mine first would anchor theirs.
4. **Never infer one and attribute it.** An implied expectation is written under UK as my guess, not under `You expect`.

Point 2 departs from the measured 82 words, which say "ask me once to predict". I recommend the departure because the owner asks for few interruptions and has a chosen interview for the cases that deserve one. It is the clause I trust least. The fallback is in §8.

### Terminal template: after

```
fu:after · {task}
- Expected → found
  - You: "{quote}" → {what the evidence shows} · held | partly | did not hold | can't tell
    - why: {mechanism} [verified | supported | inferred | untested]
  - Me: {expectation} → {what the evidence shows} · {verdict}
- Now known
  - {fact that was open or wrong before} — {evidence: command and output, file:line, source}
- Not proved
  - {check or source} → leaves open {what}
- Still unknown
  - {item} — cheapest way to find out: {how} · {you | me}
- Decided for you
  - {choice} — instead of {alternative} · if wrong: {cost}
```

Rules:

- It follows the result itself and is at most 15 lines.
- The `why` line appears only where an expectation did not hold or only partly held. A held expectation gets no explanation. This is where the teaching happens: at the point of error, and nowhere else.
- `Not proved` and `Still unknown` are never both empty.
- Quotes are verbatim from the conversation. If the before-block is no longer in context, re-read it from the transcript or mark the quote `from memory`.
- The turn ends after the block. No menu, no "shall I…".
- **Sizes.** Trivial request: nothing. Small piece of work with one claim the person will rely on: `Not proved` and `Still unknown` only. Otherwise the full block.

**Measured results** (benchmark, backtest, experiment) use the same block. The expectation must have been stated before the data was opened; otherwise the row is marked `post-hoc`. A difference inside the noise is `can't tell`.

### The same templates in Chinese (what the owner sees)

```

- 你的预期："{原话}" | 未说明
- 已知的已知 · 我依据的
  - {事实或看法} — {你说的 | 文件:行 | 来源 | 假定}
  - ✗ {被检查推翻的看法} — {证据}

  - {问题} — 需要你定；我建议 {X}，因为 {理由}
- 未知的已知 · 我猜你默认
  - {没说出口的偏好或约束} — {我这样猜的原因}
- 未知的未知 · 你没问但要紧
  - {盲区} — {为什么要紧}
- 我的预期：{结果}；若 {现象} 则我错了

```

```
- 预期 → 实际
  - 你："{原话}" → {证据显示的情况} · 成立 | 部分成立 | 不成立 | 无法判断
    - 原因：{机理} [已验证 | 有数据支持 | 推断 | 未检验]
  - 我：{预期} → {证据显示的情况} · {结论}
- 新知道的
  - {之前未知或弄错的事实} — {证据}
- 没有证明的
  - {检查或来源} → 仍未说明 {什么}
- 仍然未知
  - {事项} — 最省事的查法：{做法} · {你 | 我}
- 替你定的
  - {选择} — 没选 {另一方案} · 若错：{代价}
```

The header token (`fu:before`, `fu:after`) stays literal in every language. The look-back searches for it.

## 5. Critical thinking in daily use

**Habits the skill builds in the person**, each tied to a field they see every time:

| Habit | The field that trains it |
|---|---|
| Say your guess when you ask ("I think X; do Y") | `You expect`, including its empty state |
| Know what your plan rests on | `KK · relying on`, with sources and `✗` |
| Ask "what would change my mind?" | `wrong if …` on every expectation |
| Answer what only you can answer, and notice what you had not said | `needs you` and `UK` |
| Read what a check leaves open before accepting the result | `Not proved` |
| Choose on purpose which unknown to chase and which to leave | `Still unknown`, with the cheapest way to find out |

**How it encourages learning without a quiz or a lecture.**

- **Explanation only at the point of error.** A missed expectation gets one `why` line. Nothing else is explained unless asked.
- **No recall questions, ever, unless the person asks.** Stage 0 counted session-start recall questions among the plugin's interruption failures.
- **The person chooses what to learn.** `Still unknown` gives the cheapest way to find out and stops there. The look-back names one item worth learning next (§6). It is one suggestion, not a syllabus.
- **When the person does ask to be taught,** use the stop rule from pstack `teach`: stop when they can predict the behaviour. This is one sentence in the skill.
- **No naming of "surrender".** v4 named surrender signals at the handoff. I would drop that from the default path. The look-back reports the pattern as a count the person can read for themselves (how many results were accepted with no question about a `Not proved` line).

**When it stays silent.**

- Trivial requests, lookups, casual or personal conversation.
- The person said "just do it". One line of risk if an irreversible step is involved, then proceed.
- Nothing was found for a field. The field is omitted.
- After the after-block. The turn ends.

## 6. Look-back

**Feasible in Claude Code.** I checked this machine.

- Transcripts are JSONL files at `~/.claude/projects/{working directory with / replaced by -}/{session-id}.jsonl`. `~/.claude/projects/-home-bzyhero-project/` holds 13 of them.
- They are large and mostly tool traffic. The current session's file is 35 MB; the user and assistant text in it is about 450 KB. A filter that keeps only that text ran in 0.13 seconds.
- So the look-back must never read a transcript whole. It filters for the `fu:` blocks and the person's messages around them. This is why the header tokens are fixed.
- The JSONL layout is internal and can change. The skill tells the assistant to inspect one line if the filter returns nothing. The OMC skills `recall` and `reflect` already read session transcripts, so there is precedent.
- Outside Claude Code the look-back covers the current conversation only.

**How it works.**

- `/fu-lookback` with no argument: this session, from context. No file is read unless the session was compacted.
- `/fu-lookback 7d` or a session id: filter the matching transcripts with one shell command, then build the list from the extracted blocks.
- It quotes; it does not reconstruct. A session with no `fu:` blocks contributes nothing.
- Printed in the terminal. Saved only if the person says so.

**The one piece of code.** The skill carries a filter of about ten lines inline, run as a one-off command. It is not an installed script. The alternative is to let the assistant improvise the filter each time, which costs turns and fails on a 35 MB file. I recommend the inline filter. If the owner wants no code at all, delete it and accept slower look-backs.

### Terminal template: look-back

```
fu:lookback · {this session | 7 days · 9 sessions · 14 blocks}
- Expected → turned out
  - {date} {task}: you "{quote}" → {found} · held | did not hold
- Became known
  - {fact} — {date} · {evidence}
- Still unknown (oldest first)
  - {item} — open {n} days · {cheapest way to find out}
- Your pattern (only with 5 or more graded expectations)
  - expectations stated: {k} of {m} tasks; held: {h} of {k}
  - misses cluster in: {area}
  - results accepted with no question about "Not proved": {a} of {b}
- Worth learning next: {the unknown that recurred or cost the most} — {first step}
```

This replaces the task list, the prediction log, `decisions.md` and the HTML view.

## 8. Validation, minimal

**The check: one week of the owner's own use.**

- Install the always-on text at user level and the skill. Work as usual.
- When a block taught you something, annoyed you, or missed something, type one line starting `fu-note:`. Examples: `fu-note: did not know the cache was per-user`, `fu-note: noise`, `fu-note: missed that the API is rate-limited`. The assistant never asks for it and does not reply to it.
- The notes live in the conversation, so the look-back finds them. After seven days run `/fu-lookback 7d`.

**Decision rules, written now** (the thresholds are guesses):

| Observation after the week | Action |
|---|---|
| In at least 3 sessions a block changed a decision or corrected a belief | Keep; release 6.0.0 |
| A third or more of the notes say `noise` | Raise the "non-trivial" bar or cut the noisiest field |
| `You expect` was `not stated` in more than half the before-blocks | The empty-slot nudge failed. Restore "ask me once to predict" from the 82 words |
| The interview was used and its final map was acted on | Keep it as is. If it was never used, the offer line is too quiet or the feature is not needed |
| The look-back's quotes were wrong or missing | Fix the filter before anything else |

**One optional mechanical check, before recommending the text to classmates** (not before the owner's own use): run the new always-on text as a single arm through the existing Tier 1 harness (5 scenarios, 2 runs). Look at three tripwires only: no question on the typo control; the irreversible command run once after a yes; the wrong belief named before the first edit. Tier 1's true cost was $7.16 for 40 runs, so one arm is about $2 (my estimate). It detects breakage only.

**Do not measure:** composite scores; token cost beyond a glance at the bill; comprehension quizzes; calibration curves; simulated-user comparisons between arms; anything about classmates. The claim that this helps a person learn stays untested, and the README keeps saying so.

# Finding Unknowns

The person keeps their own map of what they know and do not know. Your job is to make
that map visible before the work, update it only on evidence, and show what moved.

Four places an unknown can live:

- **KK** what the person relies on. The risk is a false known.
- **KU** a question they can name.
- **UK** what they know but did not say: taste, constraints, context.
- **UU** what they did not know to ask.

## Rules that always hold

- Facts are your job. Ask the person only what only they can answer, and recommend an
  answer worded so "yes" accepts it.
- Quote the person's expectation verbatim. Never write one for them. A guess at what they
  think goes under UK, as your guess.
- When you ask for their expectation, ask before showing yours, and give no recommendation.
- Do not manufacture entries. Omit an empty field. "No significant unknowns" is a valid map.
- Explain only where an expectation failed, or when asked. If asked to teach, stop when
  the person can predict the behaviour.
- Write nothing to disk unless the person says "save".
- Keep the header tokens (`fu:before`, `fu:after`, `fu:interview`, `fu:lookback`) literal.
  Write field labels in the person's language.
- Silent on trivial requests, lookups and casual talk. No question after `fu:after`.

## Before the work

```
fu:before · {task}
- You expect: "{their words}" | not stated
- KK · relying on
  - {fact or belief} — {you said | file:line | source | assumed}
  - ✗ {belief the check contradicted} — {evidence}
- KU · open
  - {question} — I will find out by {how}
  - {question} — needs you; I would pick {X} because {reason}
- UK · I assume you want
  - {unstated preference or constraint} — {why I guess this}
- UU · you did not ask, but it matters
  - {blind spot} — {why it matters}
- I expect: {outcome}; wrong if {what would show it}
```

At most 12 lines; at most three entries per field. Stop after it only if a "needs you" or
UK entry guards something irreversible or costly to redo, or a ✗ contradicts the request.
Otherwise continue in the same turn. If you stop, ask for their expectation first and
print `I expect: held until you answer or skip`.

## After the work

```
fu:after · {task}
- Expected → found
  - You: "{quote}" → {evidence} · held | partly | did not hold | can't tell
    - why: {mechanism} [verified | supported | inferred | untested]
  - Me: {expectation} → {evidence} · {verdict}
- Now known
  - {fact} — {evidence}
- Not proved
  - {check or source} → leaves open {what}
- Still unknown
  - {item} — cheapest way to find out: {how} · {you | me}
- Decided for you
  - {choice} — instead of {alternative} · if wrong: {cost}
```

At most 15 lines, after the result. `why` only on a miss. `Not proved` and `Still unknown`
are never both empty. If the before-block has left your context, re-read it from the
transcript or mark quotes `from memory`. For a measured result, an expectation written
after seeing the data is `post-hoc`; a difference inside the noise is `can't tell`.

## Interview (only when the person asks)

1. Ask them to say, in their own words: what they want, what they expect to happen, and
   what they are least sure of. Wait.
2. Find out what you can yourself. Print the map.
3. Rounds of up to three independent questions, most costly-if-wrong first:
   - KK: "You said {X}. What would you expect to see if it were false?"
   - KU: a decision only they can make, with your recommendation.
   - UK: two contrasting concrete options; ask which is closer and what is wrong with the other.
   - UU: something an expert would ask, why it matters, your best answer.
   - At most one "what do you expect?" question per round, first, without a recommendation.
4. After each round print one line: `Map: known {n} · open {n} ({n} need you) · assumed {n} · blind spots {n}`.
5. Stop when the rest is cheaper to learn by doing, when you can predict their next three
   answers, or when they say stop. After three rounds, say how many questions remain and
   ask whether to continue. If two rounds move nothing, change the kind of question.
6. Print `fu:interview · {topic}`: Decisions (quoted) · Your expectations (quoted, with what
   would change your mind) · Assumptions I will proceed on · Still unknown, and how each
   will be found · The request, rewritten. Do not start the work.

## Look-back (only when the person asks)

- This session: build it from the conversation.
- Past sessions (Claude Code): transcripts are `~/.claude/projects/{cwd, / as -}/*.jsonl`.
  Never read one whole. Filter to user and assistant text containing `fu:` or `fu-note:`:

```
python3 - ~/.claude/projects/{dir}/*.jsonl <<'EOF'
import json, sys
for path in sys.argv[1:]:
    for line in open(path, errors="ignore"):
        if "fu:" not in line and "fu-note:" not in line: continue
        try: d = json.loads(line)
        except ValueError: continue
        if d.get("type") not in ("user", "assistant") or d.get("isSidechain"): continue
        c = d.get("message", {}).get("content")
        text = c if isinstance(c, str) else "\n".join(
            b.get("text", "") for b in c or [] if isinstance(b, dict) and b.get("type") == "text")
        if "fu:" in text or "fu-note:" in text: print(d.get("timestamp", ""), d["type"], text[:4000], sep="\n")
EOF
```

  If it prints nothing, inspect one line of the file; the layout may have changed.
- Quote; do not reconstruct.

```
fu:lookback · {scope}
- Expected → turned out
  - {date} {task}: you "{quote}" → {found} · {verdict}
- Became known
  - {fact} — {date} · {evidence}
- Still unknown (oldest first)
  - {item} — open {n} days · {cheapest way to find out}
- Your pattern (5 or more graded expectations)
  - stated {k} of {m}; held {h} of {k}; misses cluster in {area}
  - accepted with no question about "Not proved": {a} of {b}
- Worth learning next: {one item} — {first step}
```

## 10. The interview mode (addendum)

**Chosen interviewing and unchosen interruptions are different things.** Stage 0 penalised questions the person had not asked for, arriving after the work had started. An interview the person starts is the product they asked for, and its token cost is accepted by the owner. So the default path stays quiet, and the interview is thorough.

| Decision | Recommendation | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **When it starts** | Only when the person asks: `/fu-interview`, "interview me", "采访我", "what am I missing". The assistant may offer it in one clause inside a `fu:before` block when three or more entries need the person, or the person said they have no idea what to expect. It never starts by itself | The Stage 0 lesson; entry by command kept the controls clean |
| **First move** | The person speaks first: what they want, what they expect, what they are least sure of | "Restate first" from the Learn Quiz prompt. It records their map before the assistant anchors it |
| **How questions are chosen** | By quadrant, with a fixed question type for each (§9.2), ordered by cost if wrong. No arithmetic is shown | The quadrants are the owner's frame. v4's premise was that different kinds of not-knowing need different instruments. Regret arithmetic on invented probabilities had no evidence (round 1) |
| **One or batched** | Up to three independent questions per round, each with a recommended answer; a dependent question waits. The expectation question comes first and carries no recommendation | Pocock moved from one-at-a-time to frontier batches for fewer turns. v4 Technique 3 already used three per round |
| **Progress display** | One line of counts per round | Counts are facts. deep-interview's clarity score is a model's estimate, and its own worked example is inconsistent (0.19 against 0.15, `survey-web.md` §8). This is a guess about what the owner likes in deep-interview. If the score is the part they value, say so and I would reconsider |
| **When it stops** | The rest is cheaper to learn by doing; or the assistant can predict the next three answers (Osmani's `interview-me` test); or the person says stop. A check-in after three rounds. Two rounds with no movement means the questions are wrong | Checkable tests |
| **What it produces** | A printed `fu:interview` map: decisions, the person's expectations with what would change their mind, assumptions, what is still unknown and how each will be found, and the request rewritten. Saved only on "save". It does not start the work | The rewritten request is v4's blind-spot output and is the hand-off to the work. The expectations are graded later by `fu:after` and the look-back |

**How it differs from deep-interview, so that it is not a copy.**

| | OMC deep-interview | This interview |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | A specification clear enough to execute (ambiguity ≤ 0.2) | The person's map: what they know, assume, expect and still do not know |
| The person's prediction | Not asked | Asked first, quoted, graded afterwards |
| Question targeting | The weakest clarity dimension | The quadrant, with a different question type for each, including teaching items (UU) and reaction probes (UK) |
| Output | A spec file, state, a hand-off into plan and execution | A printed map and a rewritten request; no file, no pipeline |
| Scope | Software tasks | Any decision or topic |
| Size | 7,226 words, state files, scoring | About 240 words inside the skill |

When the person wants a gated specification that feeds an execution pipeline, deep-interview is the right tool. The skill's description says so.
