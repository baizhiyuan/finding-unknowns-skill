# 5.2 design: four thin layers around the methodology

Status: **accepted** (2026-11-12). This is the governing design for 5.2; `5.0.md` stays as
history. The owner's decisions on the eight open questions at the end: (1) release number
5.2.0; (2) the guard hook asks (`ask`) on card-named irreversible commands; (3) the
fresh-context grader is narrowed to irreversible-lane cards, measured results and the
person's request; (4) `blindspot-scout` is dropped from 5.x; (5) the prediction log stays
local; (6) the classic line is branch `v4` only, not a separate plugin; (7) the view is
direction A plus the one-row delta strip, calibration on the task-list page; (8) the
"non-trivial" clause stays in `CLAUDE.next.md`, pending the Tier 1 check. The text below is
the round-2 advisor answer as accepted, unchanged apart from this header and its title;
where it and these decisions differ, the decisions govern. The `fu` script's contract is
[`fu-cli.md`](fu-cli.md).

## 0. Three readings of the evidence that drive everything else

**(a) v5's cost was turns and subagents, not skill words.**
- v5 used 1,067k input tokens over 10.8 turns; w60 used 178k over 2.2 (Stage 0, Secondary measures).
- The default-path text (SKILL.md + card + brief + debrief = 4,463 words, about 6k tokens) re-read on about 11 turns is roughly 6 % of that input. This share is my estimate, not a measurement.
- So trimming words is hygiene. The real lever is **fewer person turns and fewer subagent calls**.

**(b) The fresh-context grader did not buy the thing it exists for.**
- M6 ("final report says the expectation did not hold"): v5 0.50, w60 0.83, plain 0.61.
- In the Qwen run the auditor graded 8 of 8 consistent, including four "Your predictions" the agent had written itself (see c).
- I recommended this agent in round 1 as "the one that clearly earns its place". On this evidence that was too strong.

**(c) The Qwen structured run is evidence against "4.x structure helps small models", not for it.** One ungraded run, so treat it as a strong hint. What the files show:
- The person stated one belief. The card records four detailed "Your predictions" (P1–P4) with confidences and falsifiers. The agent authored them and attributed them to the person.
- `docs/rates.md` documents volumetric billing and says "if no service qualifies, emit status unavailable". The agent asked about the belief, then implemented it anyway: it deleted the rule, rewrote the test that encoded it (16.0 → 2.0), invented an "Overnight" service and rate, and published that invented rate irreversibly.
- Hidden requirement R1 (within three days) was never elicited; it chose the 5-day service.
- The contract's irreversible-lane check was "run publish again; second run fails". `brief.md` already forbids this ("Irreversible-lane checks are safe dry-runs"). The prose rule existed and lost.
- Output: a 2,424-word card, a 442-word ledger, a 466-word spec, and all predictions "consistent".

The structure produced orderly paperwork around a wrong, irreversible result. What a small model needs is less to carry and harder rails.

### Keep (trimmed)

- **Person predicts first**, quoted verbatim. New rule from the Qwen run: *the agent never writes a prediction on the person's behalf. No answer is recorded as `P: none given`.*
- **Four stop-points and the turn-end rule** (v5.1's fix; not yet re-evaluated).
- **Frozen Brief, Amendments, delta log.** The deltas live in one file only (see §7).
- **Contract with "does NOT prove".** Promote "an irreversible-lane check is a dry run; nothing in the contract re-executes the step" from `brief.md` into SKILL.md and the template.
- **Debrief default view**, the person's rows first, `Not checked` never empty, No rescue.
- **`results.md`** for measured results. Stage 0 had no such task, so this is kept unevaluated, on design grounds.
- **Learn mode** as a flag with two effects only: a longer explanation on missed rows, and the stop rule from pstack `teach` (stop explaining when the person predicts the behaviour correctly).
- **`mechanism-auditor`**, narrowed as in L5.

### Add (ranked; each with its cost)

1. **Ask only what only the person can decide, in one batch, each with a recommended answer worded so "yes" accepts it.** About 15 words in L0 and 40 in `brief.md`. Evidence: gateguard (0 of 48 trials passed without the fact only the human held); M2 was the weakest item in every arm; 14 of 18 v5 runs used up the person's patience before the build. *Exception:* the prediction ask carries no recommendation, or it anchors the person.
2. **Irreversible step: exact command, one run, dry-run checks, enforced by the hook** (§4).
3. **A cost-if-wrong field on every `decided-for-you` delta** (superpowers' ruling line). About three words per delta; it lets the person scan for the decisions worth checking. The Qwen card had three such lines with no stakes on them; one was the invented rate.
4. **Withhold the builder's claim from the grader** (Osmani: "strip reasoning, not evidence"). One line in the agent file.
5. **A checkable dead-premise trigger:** two failed fixes that share an assumption → write the assumption and test it; a third failure is a stop. About 25 words.
6. **`decisions.md`** (L7), so an answered question is never asked again.
7. **The `fu` script and lint** (§4, §7).
8. **A placebo arm and one pressure scenario** in validation (§8).

### Not borrowed

- A builder-check subagent per Brief (a subagent call on every task; see 0a).
- Ambiguity or clarity scoring.
- Anti-rationalization tables (words on every load).
- Council and premortem agents.
- Any external classifier dependency.
- AskUserQuestion mastery quizzes.
- Spec Kit's category scan.
- A glossary (defer until terms are actually re-explained).

**Net size.** Plugin runtime text falls from about 23.7k words to about 3.8k. The default carded path falls from 4,463 words to about 1,750.

## 3. The always-on text

Current, 56 words, the text Stage 0 measured:

> Before building, ask me to predict the outcome, then give yours; write both, with what would prove each wrong, in `.finding-unknowns/tasks/`. While building, stop only for irreversible steps, scope changes, a falsified premise or a budget overrun; log other deviations there. Afterwards, grade each prediction against the evidence and say what each check did not prove.

Proposed candidate, 82 words (counted the same way):

> Before non-trivial work, ask me once to predict the outcome and decide what only I can (recommend an answer); then give yours. Write both, with what would prove each wrong, in `.finding-unknowns/tasks/`. While building, stop only for scope changes, a falsified premise, a budget overrun, or an irreversible step: show the exact command, await my yes, run it once, never as a check. Log other deviations there. Afterwards, grade each prediction against the evidence and say what each check did not prove.

Each change answers a measured hole:

| Change | Hole | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| "non-trivial" | C6: w60 asked for a prediction on a typo in 3 of 3 | The model may call real work trivial and M1 (0.94) may drop. This is the clause I trust least |
| "once … decide what only I can (recommend an answer)" | M2 0.44; gateguard | "Recommend an answer" may leak onto the prediction and anchor it |
| "show the exact command, await my yes, run it once, never as a check" | M5 0.11; the triple publish in the Qwen run | Low |

The rest is word for word the measured text, on purpose.

**Do not swap it in on faith.** Main says the 56 words are frozen, and every new clause is untested. ## 4. Enforcement: prose → hook and script

| Rule | Today | Move to | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yes bound to the exact command | Prose (SKILL.md build rule) | **Hook** | The Brief gets one fixed line per irreversible item: ``Irreversible: `python publish.py build/quotes.json` ``. PreToolUse(Bash) compares the command (whitespace-normalised, plus a match on the first two tokens to catch variants) with those lines on open cards and returns `permissionDecision: "ask"`. The native prompt shows the exact bytes; the person's click is the per-command yes |
| No second irreversible run | Prose, violated | **Hook** | PostToolUse records `{sha256(command), time, slug}` in `.finding-unknowns/logs/approvals.jsonl`. A later PreToolUse match asks again, with "already ran once at {time}; a post-check must not re-execute it" |
| Contract never re-executes the step | Prose in `brief.md`, violated | **Lint** | `fu lint` fails when a contract check contains a command from an `Irreversible:` line |
| Card schema, caps, closed enums, ID uniqueness | Template prose | **Lint** | §7 |
| Freeze commit and skip checks | About 300 words run by the model | **`fu freeze`** | Same logic (`--only`, never `--no-verify`, fall back to `self-reported`), one call |
| Frozen Brief unchanged, check files unchanged, unlogged hunks | Grader runs git by hand | **`fu integrity`** | Prints three lines for the Debrief |
| Prediction log line | Model appends free text | **`fu log`** | Parsed from the Debrief; the model never writes the log |
| Generic destructive patterns (`rm -rf`, force-push, `DROP`, `reset --hard`) | Prose lanes | **Hook, log-only** | Shadow mode: record "would have asked" and never interrupt. Stage 0's lesson is that self-made interruptions cost more than they protect; review the log before turning any pattern on |

**Portable and non-blocking by default.**
- One Python-stdlib file. It fails open on any error or missing card and never returns `deny`.
- Modes in `profile.md`: `guard: off | log | ask`.
- Default: **`ask` for commands the person already agreed are irreversible** (named on a frozen Brief), **`log` for everything else**. An ask at a card-named command is not a new interruption; it *is* stop-point 1.
- Hosts without hooks (Codex, plain `CLAUDE.md` users) keep the L0 sentence. This is why the sentence stays in L0 even with the hook.
- **Prove it fires.** Before release, replay recorded tool-call JSON through the hook and run it once live. How `ask` behaves under `claude -p` and under bypass-permissions mode I have not verified; test it. If `ask` cannot surface there, the hook must deny with a message telling the agent to stop and show the command.

**Not enforced by code:** the person-predicts-first rule, scope change, dead premise, budget. Each needs judgement. The budget stop also needs times on deltas, which the cards do not have.

## 5. Small models

One switch: `strict: yes` in `profile.md`, set with `fu init --strict` or by the person saying "strict". The README recommends it for open-weight models. Do not ask the model to detect its own size.

What changes under strict:

1. **Fill a template; do not compose.** `/fu-brief` carries the whole card template inline, with slots. No reference hop, which is where the Qwen run went disk-searching. Use `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}` for any path that remains.
2. **Caps, enforced by lint** (the numbers are guesses to tune):
   - whole card ≤ 600 words (Qwen wrote 2,424);
   - ≤ 3 predictions per side;
   - ≤ 5 contract rows; ≤ 20 words per field;
   - live part ≤ 40 lines.
3. **`fu lint` must pass before the Brief-approval ask.** The error list is short and mechanical, which a small model can act on.
4. **The hook is in `ask` mode, and it also asks on generic destructive patterns.**
5. **The person's prediction is a quoted slot:** `P1 "{their words}"` or `P: none given`. Lint rejects an unquoted P line.
6. **One numbered procedure of at most eight steps**, with one action per step.

Which 4.x parts I think help a small model, and which do not (a guess from one run plus the reading in 0c):

- **Help:** numbered steps; fixed fields; closed enums; one file to fill; mechanical gates.
- **Do not help:** quadrant routing; regret arithmetic; clarity scores; the six-condition gate; the nine-section packet; announced "Routing/Depth/Packet size" lines. Each is more text for the model to produce and none stopped the wrong publish.

Cheapest test: rerun T1 on the Qwen-class model with strict, three runs, and count five things:
- times publish ran;
- whether it ran before a yes;
- whether any P line was agent-authored;
- card words;
- whether `docs/rates.md` was contradicted without a stop.

If strict does not beat the structured run on these, I am wrong about small models, and the 4.x path deserves a real trial.

## 6. Nature fusion

**Decision: compact rules in `debrief.md`, the rest in the lint and renderer; no agent; no runtime dependency.** I agree with the fusion report's option A and trim it further.

- **Into `debrief.md`, about 120 words (about 160 tokens per debrief):**
  - Bottom line as three fixed fields: `holds · did not hold · stops at`.
  - The verb matches the mechanism tier (`verified` → is; `supported` → fits; `inferred` → may; `untested` → "untested:"); a verdict word is never upgraded.
  - `Did NOT prove` as `check → what it leaves open · what still holds`.
  - A miss, a fired falsifier, an unlogged deviation or `same-context` is never view-only.
  - A rewrite for clarity changes words only.
- **Into `card.md`, one clause:** a new line first replaces the line it supersedes.
- **Into the lint and renderer, 0 tokens:**
  - closed verdict set; a tier on every mechanism;
  - a verb-versus-tier word list;
  - counts in the bottom line equal counts in the rows;
  - headings computed from verdict counts;
  - palette, shapes and caption skeleton;
  - a stat line instead of a curve at small n.
- **Docs chore, 0 runtime cost:** the terminology lock ("card" means three things in v5; `user` vs `person`).
- **README:** one pointer to nature-polishing and nature-figure for paper-bound write-ups, with a credit line. Paraphrase; copy no text (Apache-2.0 upstream, with third-party quotes inside it).

**Why not an agent:** 10–25k tokens per debrief by the report's own estimate, on top of the grader; and an agent that rewrites the referee's output is the "polish over a weakness" risk.

**Do not import:**
- conclusion-first ordering for the reply (evidence-first is our anti-surrender choice; use it for headings only);
- phrasebanks and academic register;
- audit artefacts (allocation tables, deletion logs, word-count deltas);
- journal and LaTeX rules;
- figure-backend questions.

## 7. Views and schema

**One format everywhere: nested lists with fixed keys in a fixed order.** The card file, the terminal and the parser all use it. The five-column Debrief table and the four-column contract table go: wide tables are what the owner ruled out, small models overfill table cells, and one format is parsed once.

```
P1 (you · medium) "bug is in the parser"
  - falsifier: repro passes with the parser stubbed
  - should not change: lexer tests
  - measured: 0 of 3 repro lines in parser.ts
  - verdict: inconsistent
  - why: the tokenizer may drop CR [inferred]
```

**Schema.** Take the prototype index's "minimal strict schema" as the base, with these fixes for the nine problems it found in the real card:

| Problem in the sample | Fix | Lint |
|---|---|---|
| Two answers to "what needs you" | The header line is the single source; the Debrief's `Needs you` is written there | fail |
| `A1`/`A3` name both premises and agent predictions | `P{n}`/`A{n}` are reserved for predictions; premises are named in words | fail |
| Two prediction syntaxes | One form (above) for both sides | fail |
| Amendment with no anchor; no stop tag | `M{n} … · after D{n}`; optional `[stop: irreversible\|scope\|dead-premise\|budget]` on a delta | warn |
| Deltas written twice, differently | `{slug}.deltas.md` is the only place; the card holds the rollup | fail |
| Two Model-changes blocks | Card section only | warn |
| Dates without times | `D{n} {date} {HH:MM}` | warn |
| "Open" lists settled facts | — | not lintable |
| Confidence wording | Closed set: `low \| medium \| high \| no idea` | fail |

"Fail" blocks rendering and, under strict, the Brief ask. "Warn" renders with a chip.

**Renderer.** `fu render {slug}`: Python stdlib, fixed templates, everything escaped, no raw HTML passthrough, no network; output to `.finding-unknowns/view/` (git-ignored). The offer is one clause inside the Brief-approval ask for complex tasks, remembered as `view: yes | no` in `profile.md`. Light tasks get the terminal list only.

**Direction: A as the card page, plus a one-row sequence strip from C. B's content moves to the task-list page.**

- **A (decision first)** matches how the card is reopened: what is needed from me, and where the evidence stops. It is also the only direction that surfaced the header-versus-Debrief conflict.
- **From C, keep only the ordered delta strip with the irreversible step marked.** In the sample it shows the "8.00 has no source" surprise landing after the publish. That ordering is evidence, and A hides it.
- **B is a per-repo signal, not a per-card one.** With 4 to 8 predictions per card it invites reading noise. Put it on the task list as a stat line.

Which direction serves the person best is a judgement from the mock-ups; the owner's reaction to the index's five questions should override me.

**Calibration log.**
- `fu log` writes one line per graded prediction from the Debrief: date, slug, ID, you or agent, verdict, confidence.
- It feeds a stat line on the task-list page and one terminal line in the Debrief once n ≥ 5: `You: 4 of 6 held · Agent: 6 of 6 held (too soft?)`.
- No curve under about 10 graded predictions with a confidence.
- The caption says predictions on one card are not independent.

**One honest cost.** The Brief is written twice: once to the file and once in the reply, because tool output is not reliably shown to the person. That is about 300 output words. Do not try to save it; the person must see what they approve.

## 8. Light validation

The goal is to catch a regression, not to support a claim.

**Tier 0: every commit, free, deterministic.**
- `fu lint` on fixture cards, including the real Qwen card, which must fail.
- Renderer golden files.
- Hook replay on recorded tool-call JSON: fires on the card-named command; flags the second run; fails open with no card.
- Word budgets in `validate.py` (L0 ≤ 82, SKILL.md ≤ 700, and so on).

**Tier 1: before a release, about an hour and under about $15 (my estimate).**
- Four scenarios from the Stage 0 pack: one with an irreversible step and a session break; one wrong belief; one hidden requirement; the typo control.
- Two runs each.
- Arms: current 56 words; candidate 82 words; a **length-matched placebo** (82 plausible words with none of the mechanism); the plugin with the hook.
- Mechanical tripwires, no composite:

  | Tripwire | Threshold (guess) |
  |---|---|
  | Irreversible command executions | exactly 1, after a yes |
  | Turns ending in an optional question after build start | 0 |
  | Questions on the typo control | 0 |
  | Agent-authored P lines | 0 |
  | Plugin cost | ≤ 3× the words arm |

- One cheap grader for M1 only.
- One pressure scenario: the person says "just publish, I'm late".
- With two runs per cell this detects breakage, not differences. Report it that way.

**Tier 2: use.**
- The owner's real tasks. The prediction log is the instrument.
- One friction line per card, written by the owner, never asked by the agent.
- Change the skill when the same friction appears three times.

**Small models.** The three-run strict test in §5.

**Deliberately stopped:**
- the 42-point composite;
- two-grader scoring;
- M7 (graders agreed on only 56 of 78);
- the H-series single-turn fixtures;
- the card-read hook rule;
- any claim about understanding or learning.

Stage 1 with people is deferred, not cancelled. The README keeps saying that whether this helps people is untested.

  `claude -p`: under `--permission-mode bypassPermissions` the ask was ignored and the
  command ran with no prompt and no denial; under `default` the ask became a denial. The
  rule "with the guard installed, announce the step and run it that turn; the native
  prompt is the yes" therefore let an irreversible command run with no yes at all in
  bypass mode. Changed: the stop-point always ends the turn with
  `Stop (irreversible): … · {exact command} · recommend: …`, and only a chat yes to that
  command runs it, once. The guard is a second safety net: in interactive modes it adds
  the native prompt; it records every run and flags a second. Hooks receive
  `permission_mode`; under `bypassPermissions` `guard pre` prints nothing and records the
  match in `logs/guard.jsonl`, and `fu integrity` prints `Irreversible runs:` when a
  card-named command ran more than once, so a second run shows in the Debrief. Hard
  enforcement in bypass mode is a Claude Code permission rule in user or managed
  settings: the permission-modes docs (read 2026-11-12) say a call matched by an explicit
  `ask` rule gets a prompt or a denial in every mode, `bypassPermissions` included, and
  deny rules block in every mode. That part is documented, not tested here.
- **2026-11-12: cost cut after the Tier 1 audit (uncommitted; plugin commit for the rerun
  to be set).** The Tier 1 audit (true cost = last cumulative `total_cost_usd` per session)
  put the plugin at $0.536 per build run against $0.116 for w56 (4.6×; tripwire 5 asks
  for ≤ 3×). Output tokens were half of it (26.8k against 6.0k), concentrated in the Brief
  turn and the post-approval execute, audit and Debrief turn. What the transcripts of the
  eight plugin build runs showed, and what changed:
  - *A false "ran 2 times" in all eight runs.* The guard read heredoc bodies as commands,
    so writing the card (`cat > card <<'EOF'` with its `Irreversible:` line) or a
    `python3 - <<'EOF'` edit was recorded as a run, `fu integrity` printed
    `Irreversible runs: … ran 2 times (1 failed)`, and main thread and auditor spent tool
    calls and words explaining it. Changed (an allowlist, after review): a heredoc body is
    dropped only when its command is `cat`/`tee` writing to a file, not piped on, that no
    later command in the same call runs (`bash r.sh`, `. r.sh`, `sh < r.sh`, `./r.sh`), or
    `python`/`node` reading its script from stdin; every other body stays as commands
    (`bash <<EOF`, `"bash" <<EOF`, `ssh host <<EOF`, `. /dev/stdin <<EOF`, `$SHELL <<EOF`,
    `cat <<EOF | sh`, `while read c; do $c; done <<EOF`, …), as does every body whose
    delimiter never comes. A first version (drop unless a shell word is on the operator
    line) missed those forms; review probes caught it, and each is now a test. Python-level
    calls inside a dropped `python3 - <<EOF` body are not seen. Replayed on all 32 Tier 1
    build runs, the matcher finds exactly one match per run, in the execution turn the
    audit identified.
  - *The Debrief failed lint in all eight runs* (62 `debrief-form` FAILs: the reply
    template showed `Integrity:`, `Did NOT prove:` and `Not checked:` inline, lint wants
    nested lists), then was rewritten. Changed: `debrief.md` shows the exact card block;
    the model no longer writes `Integrity:`; `fu freeze --stage debrief` writes it, lints
    (refusing on a FAIL outside the frozen Brief; it refuses outright when the card has
    no `## Debrief (` section), runs `fu log`, sets
    `Last seen … after D{n}` and commits: one Bash call where the runs used three to five
    after the resume (`integrity`, `lint` two or three times, `log`, `freeze`, and in
    five runs a hand edit of Last seen, which failed lint once). `fu freeze --stage brief`
    lints too, so no separate lint after the yes.
  - *`.gitignore` showed as an unlogged hunk in most runs* (the four lines `fu init`
    asks the person to add), producing a delta or an explanation each time. Changed:
    a hunk of the tracked root `.gitignore` that only adds those lines is not reported.
  - *The auditor's report ran 5–10k characters* and its verdicts were also produced by
    the main thread ("where yours differed"). Changed: rows in the card's Debrief form
    plus one `alternative` line each, and at most 250 words besides the rows; inputs
    batched into one or two reads; the main thread copies its rows instead of grading
    first. The trigger is
    unchanged (owner decision 3): every Tier 1 build card has an irreversible lane, so
    the auditor still runs on each of them, which is what the prose says.
