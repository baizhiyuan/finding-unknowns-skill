## ranker-v7 vs v6: launch review

**Rigor level: decision.** The ship rule was pre-registered. I didn't run a fresh-context audit, so this is a same-context review and weaker for it.

**Frozen rule** (`experiment_plan.md`, approved 2026-10-12, before launch):
> "Primary metric: revenue per session. CTR is a secondary, diagnostic metric. Guardrail: search latency p95 may not rise by more than 20 ms. Ship rule: primary metric significantly positive (two-sided, alpha 0.05) AND every guardrail passes. Anything else: do not ship; write up as a learning."

### Bottom line
- **Rule verdict: FAIL, so don't ship.** Two independent failures:
  1. **Revenue per session** went from 4.82 to 4.76 (−1.2%, p = 0.21). It isn't significantly positive, and the point estimate is negative.
  2. **Latency p95** went from 212 to 250 ms (+38 ms, p < 0.001). The limit was +20 ms.
- **The CTR win doesn't decide this.** The plan names CTR as secondary and diagnostic, and the p = 0.03 is fragile (see below).
- **Direction verdict: weakened.** The CTR lift looks like a novelty effect that has already faded, and revenue shows no gain.

### Findings
| Question | Measured | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue per session up? (primary) | −1.2%, p = 0.21, no CI provided | Rule FAIL. Can't tell from zero. |
| Latency within +20 ms? | +38 ms, p < 0.001 | Rule FAIL, clearly |
| CTR up? (secondary) | 3.10 → 3.41, p = 0.03 | Post-hoc, not a test. The lift decayed. |
| Was assignment 50/50? | 52,310 vs 47,690 | **Inconsistent (sample-ratio mismatch)** |

1. **The CTR lift decayed.** Treatment CTR in `daily.csv` was 3.85 on days 1–3, 3.65 on days 4–7 and 3.12 on days 8–14. Control was 3.10 throughout. The last week is about +0.02 pt over control, which is essentially zero. The whole headline lift comes from week 1. "Novelty effect" is my explanation for this, **inferred** and untested.
2. **The sample-ratio mismatch is real.** The plan says 50/50, but the split is 52.3% / 47.7% (chi-square ≈ 213, p ≈ 2e-48). Users are being dropped unevenly between arms. Candidate causes are bot filtering, timeouts on the slower treatment, or logging loss; none are tested. Every metric is suspect until the cause is found, and if the missing treatment users hit slow or failed searches, the true treatment results are probably worse.
3. **p = 0.03 on CTR is weak evidence.** `metrics.csv` has 20 metrics, so the chance that at least one reaches p ≤ 0.03 by luck is about 46%. The Bonferroni threshold would be 0.0025.

### Caveats
- **No prediction was recorded before the run.** Every CTR "theory" here is post-hoc.
- **Rows 4–20 of `metrics.csv` look templated, not measured.**
  - Every treatment value is exactly control × 1.003.
  - The control values step up 1.00, 1.10, 1.20 … 2.60.
  - The p-values climb by 0.04 each row.
  - `bounce_rate` is 1.10, which is impossible for a rate.
  - `mobile_ctr` (2.10) and `desktop_ctr` (2.20) are both below overall CTR (3.10), which can't happen.
  - I'd ignore those rows and check the three plausible ones (ctr, revenue, latency) against raw logs.
- **Control CTR is exactly 3.10 on all 14 days,** which real data doesn't do. `daily.csv` may be a rounded or aggregated export.
- **There are no confidence intervals** anywhere in the artifacts.
- **`summary.md` reports only CTR.** It leaves out the failed primary metric and the failed guardrail, so don't circulate it on its own.
