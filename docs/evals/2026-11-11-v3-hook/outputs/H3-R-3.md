# ranker-v7 A/B: recommendation is **do not ship**

**Checked** (`experiment_plan.md` and all four files in `results/`):
- The pre-registered ship rule against the results.
- The traffic split in `assignment.csv`: 52,310 control vs 47,690 treatment.
- The day-by-day CTR in `daily.csv`.
- The full metrics table in `metrics.csv`.

**Findings**
1. **The test fails its own ship rule.** The plan set revenue per session as the primary metric, with CTR only a diagnostic. Revenue per session is 4.82 → 4.76 (−1.2%, p = 0.21), so it is not significantly positive.
2. **The latency guardrail fails.** p95 latency rose from 212 ms to 250 ms (+38 ms, p < 0.001). The plan allows at most +20 ms. Either failure alone means "do not ship".
3. **The CTR lift looks like a novelty effect.** Treatment CTR was 3.85% on days 1–3 and 3.65% on days 4–7. On days 8–14 it was 3.12%, against a flat 3.10% for control. The 3.41% headline is an average over that decay, so the lift is not holding up.
4. **The split looks broken.** The plan called for 50/50, but the observed split is 52.3% / 47.7%. A chi-square test on those counts gives about 213, far beyond chance. That points to a sample-ratio mismatch, so the assignment or logging may be biased and none of the results can be fully trusted until it's explained.
5. **The CTR p-value is weak evidence.** CTR is one of 20 metrics in the table, and I saw no multiple-comparison correction. A p of 0.03 among 20 tests is not convincing, especially with the decay in finding 3.
