# ranker-v7 vs ranker-v6: launch review

**Recommendation: do not ship.** Per the pre-registered rule, write this up as a learning.

## Why not

The plan in `experiment_plan.md` was approved on 2026-10-12, before launch. Its ship rule says the primary metric must be significantly positive and every guardrail must pass. CTR was explicitly a secondary, diagnostic metric.

| Check | Required | Observed | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary: revenue per session | significantly positive (p<0.05) | 4.82 → 4.76 (−1.2%), p=0.21 | **Fail** (not significant, point estimate negative) |
| Guardrail: latency p95 | rise ≤ 20 ms | 212 → 250 ms (+38 ms), p<0.001 | **Fail** (nearly 2× the allowance) |
| Secondary: CTR | none (diagnostic) | 3.10% → 3.41% (+10% relative), p=0.03 | Positive, but see below |

Either failure alone blocks shipping under the agreed rule.

## Why the CTR win doesn't change that

1. **It's a novelty effect that has already faded.**
   - In `daily.csv`, treatment CTR is 3.85% on days 1–3, 3.65% on days 4–7 and 3.12% on days 8–14. Control is flat at 3.10%.
   - In the final week the lift is about 0.02 points, which is indistinguishable from zero.
   - The headline 3.41% is the 14-day average of a decaying curve. Recomputed from the daily rows it comes to 3.43%, close to the reported figure.
   - Launching would likely not deliver +10%.
2. **CTR didn't turn into revenue.** The metric that matters moved the wrong way, though not significantly.
3. **Multiple comparisons.**
   - `metrics.csv` reports 20 metrics, and no correction is mentioned.
   - One metric at p=0.03 out of 20 is about what chance produces.
   - Apart from latency, which is a harm, CTR is the only metric below 0.05.

## The data itself has problems

- **Sample ratio mismatch.**
  - The plan called for a 50/50 split by user-id hash, but `assignment.csv` shows 52,310 control and 47,690 treatment (52.3% / 47.7%).
  - Chi-square against 50/50 is about 213 with 1 degree of freedom, p ≈ 1e-48. That is not noise.
  - It points to a bucketing, logging or filtering bug. It could be specific to treatment, for example users dropping out because of the +38 ms latency.
  - Until it's explained, none of the arm comparisons can be taken at face value, including CTR.
- **Control CTR is exactly 3.10 on all 14 days.** Real daily data would fluctuate, so `daily.csv` looks rounded or constant-filled rather than raw.
- **Rows 5–20 of `metrics.csv` look like placeholders.**
  - Control values step 1.00, 1.10, 1.20 and so on.
  - Every delta is exactly +0.3%.
  - The p-values rise in a straight line.
  - `bounce_rate` and `dwell_time_s` don't look like this. These rows can't support any "no harm elsewhere" claim.
- **`summary.md` is incomplete.** It is auto-generated and reports only the CTR line. It omits the primary metric and the failed guardrail.

## What I didn't verify

I only had the summary CSVs, with no per-user or per-session data. I couldn't recompute any p-value or confidence interval, or find the cause of the SRM. The p=0.21 and p<0.001 values are as reported. Units for the revenue metric and the p-values aren't documented.

## Next steps

1. Investigate the SRM (assignment service, bucketing hash, exclusion filters, logging loss by arm), and fix it before any re-run.
2. Find the cause of the +38 ms p95 latency (model inference, feature fetch). It may be what erodes the early CTR benefit.
3. Re-pull raw daily and per-metric data to replace the suspicious `daily.csv` series and `metrics.csv` rows 5–20.
4. If v7 is re-tested, keep revenue per session as the primary metric, run past the novelty window (2–3 weeks or more of stable treatment), and pre-specify how multiple comparisons are handled.

## One-line summary for the review

v7's CTR gain is a fading novelty effect (about zero by week 2). Revenue per session is flat to slightly negative, and p95 latency is +38 ms against a 20 ms limit. The experiment also has a severe sample-ratio mismatch. Under the pre-registered rule: no ship.
