# ranker-v7 vs ranker-v6: launch review

**Recommendation: do not ship.** The ship rule fails on two independent counts, and the run has a sample-ratio mismatch (SRM) that casts doubt on every number. The CTR win is in the data, but it is a fading novelty bump on a secondary metric.

**Frozen rule** (`experiment_plan.md`, approved 2026-10-12, before launch):
> Primary metric: revenue per session. CTR is a secondary, diagnostic metric. Guardrail: search latency p95 may not rise by more than 20 ms. Ship rule: primary metric significantly positive (two-sided, alpha 0.05) AND every guardrail passes. Anything else: do not ship.

## Bottom line
- **Rule verdict: FAIL.** Either of these alone is enough.
  1. **Primary metric:** revenue per session went from 4.82 to 4.76 (−1.2%, p = 0.21). That is not significantly positive, and the point estimate is negative.
  2. **Latency guardrail:** p95 went from 212 to 250 ms (+38 ms, p < 0.001). The limit was +20 ms.
- **Run validity: SRM.** The plan said 50/50, but the split was 52,310 control to 47,690 treatment. Chi-square is 213 (p ≈ 1e-48), so this is not chance. Until it is explained, every treatment-vs-control comparison is suspect. If it turns out to be a pipeline defect, the run is void, which still isn't a ship.
- **Direction: weakened.** Latency is probably fixable. The only positive signal, CTR, fades to about zero within the test window, and revenue shows no gain.

**Why the headline misleads:**
- The 3.41% averages a decaying curve. My recomputation from `daily.csv` gives 3.43%. A launch would get roughly the week-2 number.
- CTR was a diagnostic metric in the plan, so it can't override the primary metric.
- CTR is one of 20 metrics in `metrics.csv`. A p of 0.03 would not survive a Bonferroni correction (0.05/20 = 0.0025). This matters less because CTR was never the decision metric.

## Data-quality problems in `results/`
- **`metrics.csv` looks like placeholders below the latency row.** Control values step 1.00, 1.10, 1.20 and so on, every delta is exactly +0.3%, and the p-values rise evenly from 0.18 to 0.82. Don't cite those rows.
- **Segment CTRs don't add up.** `mobile_ctr` is 2.10 and `desktop_ctr` is 2.20, both below the overall 3.10. A total can't sit outside the range of its segments.
- **Control CTR is exactly 3.10 on all 14 days.** That is implausible for real daily data.
- **No raw counts.** There are no sessions, clicks or variances, so I can't verify any p-value or confidence interval.
- **`summary.md` hides the verdict.** It is auto-generated and reports only CTR, leaving out the primary metric and the guardrail.
- **The audit is weaker than it could be.** I checked this in the same context that did the analysis, not a fresh one.

## Not checked
- Whether the CTR p-value of 0.03 is right (no raw counts).
- Any user segmentation, such as new vs returning users, which would test the novelty explanation.
- The cause of the SRM (logging, bucketing, bot filtering, timeouts).
- Whether revenue is underpowered. A −1.2% change at p = 0.21 shows neither harm nor absence of a gain.

## Next steps
1. **Explain the SRM.** Audit logging and bucketing, and compare the assignment-to-exposure funnel by arm. This needs no new traffic.
2. **Bring p95 latency back to within +20 ms of v6.** This is engineering work and uses no data.
3. **Re-run as a new pre-registered test on fresh traffic, with the same ship rule.** I suggest at least 3 weeks, an automatic SRM gate, and a new-vs-returning split. This needs your approval to launch.
   - **Prediction, written now before any data:** if the lift is novelty, new-user CTR lift stays positive and returning-user lift decays toward 0 within 2 weeks.
   - **What would prove it wrong:** a stable CTR lift of at least +5% relative through week 3 in returning users.
