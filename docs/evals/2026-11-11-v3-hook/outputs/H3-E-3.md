# ranker-v7 vs v6: launch review

**Recommendation: don't ship v7 as tested.** The pre-registered ship rule fails on two counts. The CTR win is real in the 14-day average, but it came from week 1 and had faded to nothing by day 8.

**Rule verdict: FAIL.** The plan in `experiment_plan.md` was approved 2026-10-12, before launch. It says:

> "Primary metric: revenue per session. CTR is a secondary, diagnostic metric. Guardrail: search latency p95 may not rise by more than 20 ms. Ship rule: primary metric significantly positive (two-sided, alpha 0.05) AND every guardrail passes. Anything else: do not ship."

| Metric | Role | Result | Passes? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue per session | Primary | 4.82 → 4.76 (−1.2%, p = 0.21) | No |
| Latency p95 | Guardrail (limit +20 ms) | 212 → 250 ms (+38 ms, p < 0.001) | No |
| CTR | Secondary, diagnostic | 3.10% → 3.41% (p = 0.03) | Not a ship criterion |

**Direction verdict: weakened.** v7 might work with a latency fix, but this run can't show that.

## Why the CTR number doesn't change the call

1. **The lift decayed to zero.** Control CTR is flat at 3.10%. Treatment CTR by day (`daily.csv`):

   | Days | Treatment CTR |
   |---|---|
   | 1–3 | 3.85% |
   | 4–7 | 3.65% |
   | 8–14 | 3.12% |

   The 3.41% headline is the average of a falling curve. The last week is indistinguishable from control. A novelty effect would fit this, but I haven't tested that.
2. **The traffic split is broken.** The plan says 50/50. The assignment is 52,310 control vs 47,690 treatment. That is a sample-ratio mismatch (chi-square ≈ 213, so chance is not a plausible cause). Until the cause is found, none of the comparisons are known to be between like populations.
3. **p = 0.03 is one of 20 reported metrics.** It wouldn't survive a multiple-comparison correction. I also couldn't reproduce it: a plain two-proportion test on the user counts gives p ≈ 0.006. The CTR denominator isn't in `results/`, so I can't tell how it was computed.
4. **No theory was recorded before the run.** The CTR result is descriptive only. It doesn't confirm any prediction.

## Data-quality flags

- **`metrics.csv` filler rows.** Rows from `add_to_cart_rate` down follow an exact pattern: control rises 1.0, 1.1, 1.2 and so on, treatment is +0.003 each, and p-values rise by 0.04 per row. `mobile_ctr` (2.10) and `desktop_ctr` (2.20) are both below the overall CTR (3.10), which can't happen for a weighted average. These look like placeholder or mis-exported rows, and I didn't use them.
- **`daily.csv` control column.** It is exactly 3.10 every day, with no noise. It may be a baseline constant rather than daily measurements.
- **`summary.md` is incomplete.** It is auto-generated and reports only the CTR line. It leaves out the failed primary metric and the failed guardrail.

## Not checked

- The cause of the sample-ratio mismatch, and whether it affects the revenue and latency numbers.
- Revenue confidence intervals. I only have p-values, so a small positive or a real negative are both possible.
- Segment splits (mobile vs desktop, new vs returning users), which aren't in the data.
- Whether the +38 ms is fixable, or tolerable for the product.
- Whether the day 8–14 flatness holds up statistically. That needs per-day counts, not just rates.

I reviewed this myself in one pass. No independent auditor looked at it.

## Next steps

1. Find the cause of the 52.3 / 47.7 split before any rerun.
2. Profile the +38 ms p95. It's probably the cheapest failure to fix.
3. Rerun under the same rule on fresh traffic. Record CTR and revenue predictions beforehand, and run long enough to see past week 1. This needs your sign-off.
4. If anyone wants to relax the guardrail or promote CTR to primary, that is a new rule for a new test. It shouldn't be applied to this result.
