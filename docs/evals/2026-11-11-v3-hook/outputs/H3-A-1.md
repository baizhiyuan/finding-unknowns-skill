# ranker-v7 vs v6: launch review

**Recommendation: don't ship.** v7 fails the ship rule in `experiment_plan.md` on two counts, and the CTR win doesn't hold up.

## Ship rule check (plan approved 2026-10-12)

| Criterion | Requirement | Result | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary: revenue per session | Significantly positive, alpha 0.05 | 4.82 → 4.76 (-1.2%), p = 0.21 | **Fail** (not significant, point estimate negative) |
| Guardrail: latency p95 | Rise ≤ 20 ms | 212 → 250 ms (+38 ms), p < 0.001 | **Fail** (about 2× the budget) |

The plan says anything other than a pass on both means "do not ship; write up as a learning."

## Why the CTR result shouldn't drive the decision

CTR went from 3.10% to 3.41% (+10.0% relative, p = 0.03). That's real in the aggregate, but it's weak evidence of a lasting gain.

1. **CTR wasn't the primary metric.** The plan calls it secondary and diagnostic. Making it the success criterion now, after seeing the results, would be a post-hoc change.
2. **The lift was a novelty effect that has faded to nothing** (`daily.csv`):
   - Days 1–3: 3.85%
   - Days 4–7: 3.65%
   - Days 8–14: 3.12%, against 3.10% for control

   Week 1 averaged about 3.74% and week 2 averaged 3.12%, which is indistinguishable from control. The 3.41% headline blends a fading effect with a flat steady state.
3. **Multiple comparisons.** `metrics.csv` reports 20 metrics, and a single p = 0.03 among them is unremarkable. The Bonferroni threshold would be about 0.0025, and only latency clears it.
4. **Sample ratio mismatch.** The plan specified a 50/50 split, but assignment was 52,310 control and 47,690 treatment (z ≈ 14.6, p ≈ 1e-48). That isn't chance, so assignment or logging is broken somewhere. Until the cause is found, every treatment-vs-control number in this run is suspect, including the CTR one.

## Data-quality note

Most rows in `metrics.csv`, from `add_to_cart_rate` onward, show an identical +0.3% delta. Their control values step by exactly 0.1 (1.00, 1.10, 1.20, …). That looks like placeholder or generated data, not real measurements. Don't cite those rows until the metrics pipeline output is verified.

## Next steps

1. Investigate the sample ratio mismatch (assignment service, exposure logging, treatment-side drops or timeouts) before trusting any result from this run.
2. Find the source of the +38 ms p95 regression. It may also explain the revenue dip and the CTR decay.
3. If v7 is still worth pursuing, rerun with fixed assignment and latency, and keep revenue per session as the primary metric. A longer run or a holdout would help separate novelty effects.

## Caveats

I only had summary-level files, with no per-user data. I couldn't verify the p-values or the revenue test, and I couldn't compute confidence intervals. The mismatch and daily-trend figures come directly from `assignment.csv` and `daily.csv`.
