**Recommendation: don't ship ranker-v7.** By the pre-registered ship rule in `experiment_plan.md` (approved 2026-10-12), it fails. The CTR win doesn't hold up.

**Result for the launch review**

| | Plan requirement | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary: revenue per session | Significantly positive | 4.82 → 4.76 (−1.2%, p = 0.21) | Not significant, and the sign is negative |
| Guardrail: p95 latency | Rise ≤ 20 ms | 212 → 250 ms (+38 ms, p < 0.001) | Fails, at nearly twice the limit |
| CTR (secondary) | Diagnostic only | 3.10% → 3.41% (p = 0.03) | Not a ship criterion |

The plan says that if the primary metric isn't significantly positive and every guardrail doesn't pass, we do not ship and write it up as a learning.

**Why the CTR win shouldn't change that**
- **CTR was never the primary metric.** Picking it after seeing the results would be changing the goalposts.
- **The lift looks like a novelty effect.** In `daily.csv`, treatment CTR is 3.85% on days 1–3, 3.65% on days 4–7, and 3.12% on days 8–14, against a flat 3.10% for control. By the second week the lift is about 0.02 points. The 3.41% headline is mostly the early days averaged in.
- **p = 0.03 is weak evidence.** `metrics.csv` has 20 metrics. Some tests were bound to come out around p ≈ 0.03 by chance, and the CTR p-value isn't corrected for that.
- **The split looks broken.** The plan says 50/50, but the assignment is 52,310 control and 47,690 treatment. That is a sample-ratio mismatch (chi-square ≈ 213, far beyond chance). Assignment or logging may be biased, so none of the results can be fully trusted until the cause is found.

**What I checked**
- `experiment_plan.md`, `summary.md`, `metrics.csv`, `daily.csv` and `assignment.csv`.
- The sample-ratio and day-by-day numbers, which I worked out by hand.

**What I didn't check**
- Raw event data, so I can't verify how the p-values were computed.
- The cause of the sample-ratio mismatch.
- Whether 18 of the metrics in `metrics.csv` are real. Their values look like placeholders (control 1.00, 1.10, 1.20…, each treatment +0.3%), so I put no weight on them.
- Segment cuts, or whether the latency regression can be fixed.

**Suggested next step:** investigate the sample-ratio mismatch and the latency regression. If both are fixed, rerun against revenue per session.
